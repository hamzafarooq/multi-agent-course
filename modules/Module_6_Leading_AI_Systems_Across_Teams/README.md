# Module 6 — Leading AI Systems Across Teams

You've spent five weeks *building* AI systems — the loop, the subagents, the retrieval, the
protocols, the voice stack. This module is about *leading* them: setting the strategy, aligning
the team, driving adoption inside an organization, governing responsibly, and proving to
stakeholders that any of it moved the business.

> **New here?** This module is part of the [FDE Agent Engineering Bootcamp](../../README.md).
> Open the repo root in Claude Code and type `/start` — Claude reads `CLAUDE.md` and becomes
> your tutor. The files below are what it teaches from.

## What this module covers

| # | Topic | What you'll be able to do |
|---|-------|---------------------------|
| 1 | **Leadership & team alignment** | Build leadership skills, set shared goals, and align a team around an AI initiative. |
| 2 | **AI strategy & roadmapping** | Define AI goals, prioritize use cases, and build a practical roadmap for business impact. |
| 3 | **Organizational change & adoption** | Drive change, enable adoption, and build a culture that embraces AI transformation. |
| 4 | **AI ethics, governance & compliance** | Understand ethics, set policies, and ensure responsible and compliant AI use. |
| 5 | **Measuring impact & ROI** | Measure outcomes, track ROI, and communicate AI impact to stakeholders. |

**Lead · Plan · Adopt · Govern · Measure.**

## How it connects

- **Backward:** every architecture trade-off you made in Modules 1–5 (cascade vs. speech-to-speech,
  RAG vs. knowledge graph, one agent vs. many) is a *decision someone has to own and defend*. This
  module is where you learn to make and communicate those decisions for a team, not just a repo.
- **Forward:** Demo Day (Week 7) asks you to *evaluate* and *defend* EPYHIA, not just show it. The
  measurement and communication skills here are what turn a demo into a decision.

## Suggested exercise (until the full set lands)

Pick one system you built in Modules 1–5. Write a one-page brief for a non-technical leader:
what it does, what it costs per run, what could go wrong, what policy would govern it, and the
one metric you'd track to know it's working. That brief is the seed of every topic above.
