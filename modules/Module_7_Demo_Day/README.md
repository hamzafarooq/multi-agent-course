# Demo Day: Production-Ready AI Systems

Six modules taught the concepts. Four projects made you ship them. Demo Day is where you show
the last one, your **Capstone**: a real problem of your choosing, built and deployed. You demo it
live and defend it on the numbers.

> **New here?** This is the closing session of the [Forward Deployed Engineering Bootcamp](../../README.md).
> See [the four projects](../../README.md#the-four-projects) for how the Capstone builds on Ledger, Herald and Chorus.

## What happens on Demo Day

| Step | What it is |
|---|---|
| ▶️ **Showcase** | A live run of your Capstone doing real work for its user, with every side effect going through one action gateway. |
| 🔍 **Evaluate** | Your project is scored against its `rubric.json` by `eval.py`, not by the pitch. The Product Evaluation report you submit is the evidence. |
| 💬 **Feedback** | Peers and instructor critique the *trajectory*, not just the output: which tool calls were the right ones, which errors surfaced, why the loop stopped. |
| ✅ **Next steps** | What you'd ship next, what you'd never ship, and what it would cost at 100× the volume. |

## What you bring

- A **deployed** Capstone that passes its own eval, with the report and a 60–90s demo video.
- One **successful** trajectory and one **failing** trajectory you've read end to end, and can
  explain step by step. If you can't produce a failing one, you don't understand the failure surface yet.
- Your **budget numbers** — tokens, latency, cost per run — against what you declared before you built it.

## The bar

A thing that ran is not a thing that worked. Demo Day grades on whether the system does the
right thing for the right reasons, measurably, and whether you can name the worst bug you shipped
into and what caught it.
